# Ethics and privacy

## Principles
1. **Support, not surveillance.** Alerts invite a kind conversation. They are never used for marks, discipline, ranking or admissions.
2. **Voluntary and transparent.** The check-in is optional, explains who sees answers, and can be skipped or discarded. The only action taken without the student asking is a counsellor reaching out if answers suggest danger, and students are told this first.
3. **Minimum necessary access.** Faculty see gentle summaries ("attendance has dropped"). Only counsellors see survey detail.
4. **Human in the loop.** Lunala never diagnoses or acts alone. Screens (PHQ-2, GAD-2, WHO-5) are not diagnoses.
5. **No stereotyping.** The system does not predict risk from hobbies, interests or personality type. Groups do not predict individuals, and base rates make such predictions mostly false alarms.
6. **Safety first.** A self-harm item triggers an immediate helpline message and an Urgent route to a human counsellor.

## Why we avoid hobby-based prediction
Even a very accurate model applied to a rare outcome produces mostly false positives. Labelling students by interests also stigmatises them, discourages honest answers and can breach consent and purpose-limitation rules (for example under India's DPDP Act 2023; check with a lawyer).

## Before real deployment
Consent forms, role-based access control with audit logs, secure storage, a retention policy, licensed and locally validated scales, bias testing across groups, and a clear counsellor escalation protocol.
